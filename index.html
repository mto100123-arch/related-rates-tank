The Problem

A right cylindrical water tank with a diameter of 3 feet and a height of 6 feet is being drained. At what rate is the volume of the water in the tank changing when the water level is dropping at a rate of 4 inches per minute?
(A) −9π ft³/min
(B) −4.5π ft³/min
(C) −3π ft³/min
(D) −0.75π ft³/min

Draining a Cylindrical Tank

Diameter 3 ft → radius r = 1.5 ft. Height 6 ft. Level drops at 4 in/min.
V = πr²h = 2.25π·h
r constant ⇒ dV/dt = 2.25π·dh/dt
dh/dt = −4 in/min = −1/3 ft/min
dV/dt = 2.25π(−1/3) = −0.75π ft³/min
The rate is the same at every level. Drag the slider and watch it stay fixed.
depth h = 6.00 ft

Step-by-Step Solution

1. Write the volume equation.
V = πr²h
The tank is a right cylinder, so its volume is area of the circular base times the water depth.
2. Substitute the constant radius.
The diameter is 3 ft, so r = 1.5 ft and r² = 2.25. The radius never changes as the tank drains, so it is a constant, not a variable:
V = 2.25π·h
This turns a two-variable problem into a single-variable one. Nothing involving dr/dt will ever appear.
3. Differentiate with respect to time.
dV/dt = 2.25π · dh/dt
The constant 2.25π just rides along; implicit differentiation of a constant times h is ordinary.
4. Convert units.
dh/dt = −4 in/min = −4/12 ft/min = −1/3 ft/min
The classic trap. The answer choices are in cubic feet per minute, so the rate must be in feet per minute. The minus sign records that the level is dropping.
5. Multiply.
dV/dt = 2.25π · (−1/3) = −0.75π ft³/min
Negative because the tank is losing water. Answer (D).

The insight most students miss

Because the radius is constant, the rate of volume change does not depend on how full the tank is. "When the water level is dropping..." sounds like it should matter, but for a cylinder it doesn't: dV/dt is −0.75π ft³/min at h = 6 ft, at h = 3 ft, at h = 1 inch. The phrase "when..." would only matter for a shape whose cross-section changes with height, like a cone or a sphere.
